# Faery Tale Blues, Take Two

Welcome to our set, Faery Tale Blues, Take Two. If we're not the line-up you originally booked to see, thank you for keeping faith.

If you're familiar with fairy tales, that's f-A-I-r-y tales, you'll know they have often have a similar structure. Born in adversity, some highs, some lows, some challenges along the way, often providing subversive solutions to political problems, and then all wrapped up in a happy ending. Usually. Because a fairy tale ending, in stories and in real life, is often taken as meaning "happily ever after".

And if you know anything about the fae, the faerie- that A-E — folk, the Good People of Other people, the Tywlth Teg, of Wales, the Piskies of Cornwall, the fey folk of many other traditions, you'll know that they live in a world parallel to ours, though occasionally our paths can cross. These sorts of tale are sometimes explanatory, often cautionary, voicing concerns, speaking around taboos, or commenting on behaviours of ours which are unacceptable to them; or behaviours of theirs which are unacceptable to us. There is one story that tells of an elderly midwife, called at dead of night to deliver a baby, a baby onto whose eyes she must rub a magical ointment. She accidentally gets some of the ointment in one eye, and the world of faery is revealed to her. But when she reveals this insight to one of the faeries, she is punished, and loses sight in that eye altogether.

This set started out as one thing, and has become another. What we bring to you today is hopefully in keeping with the title, and something of the original inspiration. A combination of traditional stories, traditional songs and traditional tunes, that together provide a glimpse into a parallel world, where the stories don't necessarily have a happy endings, where minutes take hours to pass, and hours take minutes. So welcome, to the world of Faery Tale Blues.
